## Pair 1 — prompt: Write a very short story about an object in a museum that remembers being used.

### A

The Dancing Shoes

In the dimly lit corner of the museum, sat a pair of old-fashioned dancing shoes. They were a deep red and had intricate embroidery around the ankles. The shoes were over a hundred years old, and many people walked past them, not noticing their faded charm.

But the shoes had a secret. They remembered every dance they had been a part of. They could still feel the soft rustle of fabric, the clink of glass as couples sipped champagne, and the soft chime of music waltzing through the air.

One night, in the late hours of the museum, the shoes felt a weight on their soles. They vibrated with excitement as they were being lifted, and then...they felt the smooth surface of human skin. A young woman had come to dance with them.

The shoes guided her, they remembered the twists and turns, the spins and sweeps, and the woman followed, spinning around the silent gallery floor. The shoes closed their eyes and let themselves remember the laughter, the joy, and the bliss of being alive. They whispered secrets to the woman's feet, and together they danced, for a moment, in the empty museum, reliving the past.

### B

The curator stared at the old, leather-bound journal in front of her, feeling the weight of centuries pressing down on her. This object in the museum's collection had a story to tell, and she could almost sense its memory.

As she carefully traced her finger over the delicate script, the ink on the pages came to life, the letters blurring and twisting before her eyes. She gasped as she saw a young woman scribbling frantically in the journal, her hand shaking as she wrote.

The scene flickered back and forth, revealing flashes of a long-forgotten time. She saw the woman in a garden, her hands covered in earth as she carefully planted a seed. She saw her standing over a bed, her eyes shining with pride as she held a newborn baby. And she saw her huddled in a corner, tears streaming down her face, clutching the journal tight as if it were a lifeline.

The curator sat back, her heart pounding in her chest. This journal wasn't just an object in a museum – it was a window into the past, a witness to history. She could almost hear the woman's voice, see the pain and joy in her eyes. And she knew that, no matter how many visitors passed through the museum's doors, this journal would remember.

## Pair 2 — prompt: Write a very short story in which someone inherits a debt of a highly unusual kind.

### A

Lily woke up to a knock on her door. She rubbed her eyes and opened the door to a man in a sharp suit holding a folder. He introduced himself as the executor of her late grandfather's will.

Lily's grandfather, a renowned astrologer, had died a year ago. She knew she would receive some money, but she never expected a bill for over a thousand cosmic surgeries.

Cosmic surgery? Lily's mind was spinning. The executor explained that her grandfather believed in altering the astrological configurations in the universe to better his clients' fortunes. He charged exorbitant fees for these so-called surgeries.

Lily's grandfather had not just inherited clients; he had left her with their debts. She felt a chill run down her spine. Lily was an IT engineer, not a fortune teller. She had bills of her own to pay, and this new debt was overwhelming.

She realized she had a decision to make: honor her grandfather's legacy and continue these cosmic surgeries, or write off this debt as an exceptionally bizarre inheritance.

Lily looked up at the sky, contemplating her grandfather's belief that astrology governed everything. As she made her choice, the stars seemed to twinkle just a little brighter.

With a deep breath, Lily decided to honor her grandfather's work, and she began to schedule cosmic surgeries for her grandfather's clients. She was shocked at how well these surgeries worked, and how quickly her clients' fortunes changed.

Lily found herself enjoying this new aspect of her life, and before she knew it, she was becoming known as the go-to astrologer. She never forgot her initial doubts about this strange legacy, but it brought her immense happiness and fulfillment, and she knew that her grandfather would have been proud.

As she looked up at the sky, she saw the stars twinkle brighter still, and she knew that her grandfather's legacy was in safe hands. Lily realized that sometimes, the most unusual legacies can lead to the greatest opportunities.

### B

As soon as Claire received the letter informing her of her grandfather's passing, she knew it wasn't going to be good news. He had always been tight with his finances, and the fact that he had summoned her all the way to South Carolina from New York City only meant one thing: trouble.

The lawyer, a wiry little man with a bald head and a serious demeanor, didn't waste any time in delivering the news. "Your grandfather has left you something, Miss Davis," he said, handing her an envelope. "But unfortunately, there's a catch."

Claire's heart sank as she read the contents of the envelope. Her grandfather, it seemed, had a... Unusual type of debt. One that couldn't be settled with money.

Apparently, he had made a deal with a local faerie several years ago. The exact nature of the deal wasn't specified, but the faerie wanted something back in return. Something that would involve Claire, and something that would take her to the depths of the Appalachian forest.

The faerie wanted a human sacrifice.

Claire's mind was reeling. Her grandfather, a devoutly religious man, had dabbled in something so twisted and ancient that she could hardly believe it. And now she was supposed to do his bidding and offer herself up to a supernatural being?

But then she remembered something her grandfather had once told her. "Always be prepared for the unexpected," he had said. "Life is full of surprises."

Claire knew she had to follow through with this, no matter how impossible it seemed. She packed a bag and set off for the forest, her heart pounding in her chest. She had no idea what to expect, but she knew one thing for certain: she wasn't going to let her grandfather's legacy end with her.

As she pushed her way through the undergrowth, she could hear the faerie's laughter echoing through the trees. She knew she was being led on a deadly game, but she had one thing on her side: determination.

In the end, she emerged from the forest with her life intact, but with a newfound respect for the mysteries of the world. Her grandfather's debt had been paid, but at what cost? She would never forget the way the faerie's eyes had glittered with malice, or the sound of his laughter. But she would also never forget the way her heart had beaten in her chest as she walked away, alive but changed forever.

## Pair 3 — prompt: Write a very short story about the first morning after a city's bells all stop.

### A

The silence was deafening.

For as long as Lena could remember, the bells of the city had been her alarm clock. They jolted her out of sleep each morning and into a frenzied rush to work. But today, there was no jolt, no rush.

She opened her eyes and listened, her heart pounding in her chest. The silence was absolute. No honking cars, no chatter of pedestrians, no clanging of construction. Just... Quiet.

Lena sat up slowly, her head spinning. She rubbed her eyes and looked out the window, squinting against the bright morning sun. The street below was deserted, except for a lone pigeon pecking at something on the ground.

She frowned and slipped on her slippers, determined to investigate. She crept downstairs, trying not to make a sound, and pushed open the door to the street.

There was no one there. Just an eerie stillness that made her skin crawl. She walked down the deserted boulevard, her footsteps echoing like gunshots. It was like a movie set, a city abandoned for some apocalyptic shoot.

But this was no shoot. This was real.

Lena reached the town square, where the towering church bell tower loomed. And there, at the base of the tower, she found the cause of the silence.

The bells had stopped ringing. All of them.

Lena stared up at the tower, her heart hammering in her chest. She couldn't imagine a world without the bells. They were a constant reminder of the city's pulse, its rhythm, its soul.

But now... Now, it was quiet. Too quiet. Lena shivered and quickened her pace, feeling a growing sense of unease. This wasn't right. This wasn't normal.

She walked back to her apartment, her footsteps echoing like gunshots. She tried the radio, but there was nothing but static. She tried the phone, but the line was dead. She was completely cut off from the world.

And then, she heard a sound. It was faint at first, like a distant murmur. But then, it grew louder, until it was a deafening roar.

It was a sound she knew all too well. It was the sound of emergency sirens.

Lena's heart leapt into her throat. She scrambled for her coat and rushed out of the apartment, her mind spinning.

She knew what this meant. She knew what was coming.

But she had no choice. She had to survive. She had to find answers. She had to... She had to face the silence.

### B

The clock on the wall blinked 12:00 in neon red, its humming motor still. The streets below lay quiet. All around the city, the bells had fallen silent, as if by command.

Alice sat up in bed, her heart racing. She clutched her pillow and listened. Not a sound in the whole world, just the rhythmic thump of her heart. The silence was almost suffocating, as if the world had turned itself off.

Suddenly, she heard a noise outside. She froze, her breath catching in her throat. The sound grew louder, heavy footfalls that shook the floorboards.

Alice crept to the window and peered outside. The streets were empty, save for one figure, dressed all in black. The figure carried something long and heavy, set it down in the middle of the road, and vanished.

Alice pressed her nose to the windowpane, her heart in her throat. The heavy object glinted in the light, a metallic ball with intricate engravings. She recognized it immediately - the clock from the town square.

The streets remained still, the city's heartbeat paused. Alice felt a shiver run down her spine. What was happening? Who had done this?

She knew she couldn't stay cooped up forever. She had to find out what was going on, had to face the unknown.

Alice dressed quickly, ignoring the empty silence, and stepped outside. The city lay before her, mute and still, like a sleeping giant. She walked towards the town square, her heart hammering in her chest.

As she reached the center, she saw the metallic sphere, and something caught her eye - it glowed, faintly at first, then grew brighter until Alice could see what it was.

It was an intricate design, etched into the clock's face. Alice frowned, unable to decipher it. She traced the pattern with her finger, her eyes following the curves and spirals.

Suddenly, Alice understood. The design was like a maze, one that could be deciphered if she followed the path. Alice's mind raced with possibilities, and she knew what she had to do.

With a deep breath, Alice traced the lines, her heart pounding in her chest. As she followed the maze, the clock began to hum, growing louder until Alice felt it in her bones.

And then, with a sudden burst of light, the bells began to ring once more. Alice stumbled backwards, her hands over her ears. The sound was so loud, so overwhelming that she could feel it in her bones.

As she walked back to her home, Alice felt a newfound sense of purpose. The bells would ring once again, but now, Alice knew the secret. She had solved the puzzle, deciphered the mystery, and saved the city from an eerie, endless silence.

As Alice reached her doorstep, she felt a sense of calm wash over her. The city's heartbeat had resumed, and Alice knew that she had found her place within it.

## Pair 4 — prompt: Write a very short story in which a lighthouse keeper receives a letter addressed to the lighthouse itself.

### A

The lighthouse had always been a solitary figure, its constant beacon of light unchanging in the ever-shifting tides. But one day, something strange arrived in its keeping. A letter addressed not to the keeper, but to the lighthouse itself.

At first, the keeper couldn't believe his eyes. But as he read the elegant script, he realized the letter was indeed meant for his charge. It was written by a woman named Eleanor, and in it, she spoke of the lighthouse as though it were alive.

"My dearest Lighthouse," she wrote. "I write to you now, on the eve of my departure. I have spent many nights at your base, gazing up at your gentle glow, and I wish to tell you that you are more than just a beacon. You are a friend."

The keeper's heart swelled as he read these words. He had never thought of the lighthouse as anything more than a job, a duty to keep the light burning. But Eleanor's letter made him see the lighthouse in a new light.

As he walked the stairs to the top of the tower, he felt a sense of reverence. For the first time, he realized that this was not just a job, but a calling. A duty to uphold the lighthouse's legacy, to keep it standing for future generations.

And so, the keeper vowed to take better care of the lighthouse, to clean it more thoroughly, and to ensure that its light never went out. He would honor Eleanor's words, and the lighthouse would continue to stand as a friend, both to him and to all who gazed upon its gentle glow.

### B

The winds whipped through the narrow passageway of the lighthouse, howling in protest as they battled against the heavy wooden doors. The lighthouse keeper, Jameson, paced the room, his footsteps echoing off the stone walls. He'd been expecting supplies to arrive any day now, but the weather had other plans. It had been days since he'd last seen another soul, and the storm showed no signs of abating.

As Jameson reached for the lantern on his mantelpiece, his hand knocked a thin envelope onto the floor. It was unaddressed, but the familiar seal of the lighthouse itself graced the front. Jameson felt a shiver run down his spine as he broke the wax.

Dear keeper of this light,

The ocean is vast and unforgiving, but its secrets are not meant for all. In the depths below, there are wonders that should be kept hidden, lost treasures and ancient mysteries. I beg of you, please do not share these secrets with the world. Keep them safe in the depths where they belong.

With deepest gratitude,

[Unsigned]

Jameson's heart pounded in his chest as he read the letter. It spoke of things he couldn't even imagine, secrets that should have remained hidden. He'd devoted his life to this lighthouse, but he couldn't help but feel as though he now had a great responsibility to safeguard the secrets beneath the waves.

The storm raged on, but Jameson's mind was lost in thought as he watched the sea churn below. The secrets of the ocean would stay that way - protected, hidden and safe in the depths where they belonged.
